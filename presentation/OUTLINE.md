# Presentation script — 4 minutes

Seven slides. Timings assume a calm pace; the hard limit is five minutes.
One idea per slide, one number per idea.

---

### Slide 1 — The decision (0:00–0:20)

> "Eniac wants into Brazil within a year. Magist offers the infrastructure to do
> it. Two objections stood in the way, and we took both to the data."

Show the two concerns as written by the business: catalogue fit, delivery speed.
Do not preview the answer yet.

---

### Slide 2 — Magist is worth taking seriously (0:20–0:50)

**99,441 orders · R$13.6m · 3,095 sellers**
Growth curve: 750 orders in Jan-2017 → 6,351 in Aug-2018.

> "Whatever we decide, we are not dealing with a small player. Order volume grew
> eightfold in twenty months."

---

### Slide 3 — Concern 1: the catalogue half-fits (0:50–1:40)

Two panels side by side.

**Left — tech is there:** 15.3 % of items, and `computers_accessories` is the
5th largest category on the platform at R$912k.

**Right — the price class is not:** median item **R$74.99**, 99th percentile
**R$890**. Only 0.75 % of items reach R$1,000.

> "A 2018 iPhone in Brazil cost three and a half to five thousand reais. On this
> platform that is past the 99th percentile. Our accessories fit perfectly. Our
> flagship hardware has no precedent here — only 202 of 3,095 sellers have ever
> shipped a single item over a thousand reais."

This is the slide the recommendation rests on. Do not rush it.

---

### Slide 4 — Concern 2: adequate, not fast (1:40–2:25)

**12.1 days average — against a 23.4-day promise.**

> "They hit their promise 92 % of the time. But look how: they promise 23 days.
> That is reliability bought with a conservative quote, not with speed."

Then the trend, which is the better news: 2018-Q1 at 15.3 days and 14.6 % late,
2018-Q3 at **7.9 days**. Halved in two quarters.

---

### Slide 5 — Why this is the real risk (2:25–3:00)

**On time → 4.29 stars. Late → 2.55 stars, and 54.6 % of those are 1–2 stars.**

> "Eniac's differentiator is the human relationship with the customer. One missed
> delivery date makes a one-or-two-star review five times more likely. This is
> where a logistics decision becomes a brand decision."

---

### Slide 6 — The recommendation (3:00–3:40)

> **"Sign — but not this deal."**

1. **Accessories first.** They sit in the R$75–230 band Magist already owns.
2. **12–18 months, not three years, with a delivery SLA.** The three-year price
   was set against 2018-Q1 — the worst quarter in the data.
3. **São Paulo and the Southeast first.** 8.3 days there, 23.8 in the North.

---

### Slide 7 — What we still need to ask (3:40–4:00)

> "One honest limit: this data shows what Magist's *sellers* do, not what Magist
> *can* do for us. Before signing, we need their delivery performance on parcels
> above a thousand reais, plus insurance and returns terms for high-value
> electronics. That is the one question the data cannot answer — and it is the
> one the deal turns on."

Ending on the open question is deliberate. It shows the analysis knows its own
boundaries, and it hands the head of Eniac a concrete next step.

---

## Delivery notes

- Say **two** numbers per slide at most. Everything else is on the slide for the
  audience to read.
- The strongest moment is slide 5. Pause after "five times more likely."
- If you run long, cut slide 2 to a single sentence — scale is the least
  contested point.
- Expect the question *"why not just test flagship hardware too?"* Answer: 203
  items in `computers` over two years means we would be the experiment, with our
  brand carrying the risk.
